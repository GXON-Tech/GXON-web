CUSTOMER BACKGROUND & CHALLENGE

Typhoon-Season Intake at Industrial Scale

Regional grain depots must process large volumes fast — typhoon-season harvests arrive wet and must be dried continuously to prevent spoilage across thousands of tons.

A Depot Under Peak-Season Pressure

A regional grain depot in the Philippines aggregates rice and maize from surrounding provinces. During the typhoon-season harvest, intake volumes spike dramatically — and grain arrives at moisture levels far above safe storage thresholds.

Batch drying cannot keep pace with the intake volume. The depot needs continuous, unattended operation that runs day and night without requiring a large operator workforce to be present at all hours.

Quality at Scale

For export and government reserve standards, moisture uniformity is critical. Direct-heat drying risks contamination and fissuring. The depot requires indirect heat exchange — where flue gas never contacts the grain — and PLC control that maintains precise temperature across all moisture levels.
