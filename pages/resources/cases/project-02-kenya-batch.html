CUSTOMER BACKGROUND & CHALLENGE

Drying Without the Grid

In off-grid regions, maize harvests arrive with moisture too high for storage — but without electricity, diesel-only drying is costly and supply-dependent.

A Cooperative Seeking Price Leverage

A farmer cooperative in Kenya aggregates maize from smallholder members across an off-grid region. Without drying infrastructure, members must sell immediately after harvest when prices are lowest — losing the leverage that storage would provide.

Maize arrives at moisture levels well above the 13% safe storage threshold and must be reduced before it can be held. The cooperative needs both drying and sealed storage to hold grain for months and sell when market prices improve.

No Grid, No Diesel Monopoly

The site has no grid connection, making electric-powered drying impossible. Diesel-only operation is costly and vulnerable to supply disruptions. The cooperative needs a multi-fuel system that can burn whatever is locally available — and a silo that doubles as storage.
