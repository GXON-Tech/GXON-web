THE CHALLENGE

When Monsoon Meets Harvest

South Asia's monsoon season aligns with the rice harvest — creating a perfect storm of high humidity, heavy rainfall, and grain that spoils within 48 hours.

Humidity That Defeats Sun Drying

During the monsoon months (June–September), ambient humidity across Bangladesh, eastern India, and Pakistan routinely exceeds 80%. Paddy harvested during this period carries 25–30% moisture content — far above the 13–14% safe storage threshold.

Traditional sun drying becomes impossible: there is no sun, and the ground is waterlogged. Grain left in these conditions begins to sprout, ferment, or develop mold and aflatoxin within 48 hours. Farmers lose 15–25% of their harvest every season.

Rice Husk: Waste or Resource?

Every rice mill in South Asia generates mountains of rice husk as a byproduct. Most mills dump or burn it inefficiently, creating pollution. But rice husk has a calorific value of 3,000–3,500 kcal/kg — making it an excellent fuel for grain drying. GXON's solution turns this waste into free fuel.

HOW IT WORKS

The Drying Process

From wet paddy to storage-ready grain in five automated steps.

1
Load Paddy
Harvested paddy at 25–30% moisture loaded into the 30-ton batch chamber via elevator.
2
Husk Ignition
Rice husk furnace ignites. Heated air is channeled indirectly — flue gas never contacts grain.
3
Auto Drying
PLC controls temperature (50–60°C) and cycle time. Dual sensors monitor air and grain temperature.
4
Grain Agitation
Auto grain agitator circulates paddy for uniform moisture reduction — no manual turning needed.
5
Seal & Store
Once moisture reaches 13–14%, the dual-use silo seals for storage. No transfer needed.

FAQ

Common Questions

How much rice husk do I need per ton of paddy?
Approximately 15–20 kg of rice husk per ton of paddy, depending on initial moisture content and ambient temperature. A typical rice mill processing 50 tons of paddy per day generates enough husk to fuel the dryer for the same volume — making fuel cost effectively zero.
Can the dual-use silo store wet grain before drying?
No. The silo should only be sealed for storage after grain has been dried to safe moisture level (≤14% for paddy). Storing wet grain will cause mold and aflatoxin growth within 48 hours. The drying cycle must complete before sealing.
What if my rice mill is too far from the drying site?
The G-SR-30 supports multi-fuel operation. If rice husk transport is not economical, the burner can be switched to diesel, natural gas, or biomass pellets. The husk furnace is a modular add-on that can be detached and reinstalled at a different location.
How long does one drying batch take?
4–6 hours per batch depending on initial moisture (25–30% down to 13–14%), ambient humidity, and grain type. The PLC auto-adjusts drying time based on real-time moisture sensor readings — ensuring consistent results regardless of weather conditions.
Is the system suitable for parboiled rice?
Yes. Parboiled paddy typically enters the dryer at 30–35% moisture after steaming. The G-SR-30's temperature control (50–60°C) is ideal for parboiled rice — preventing fissuring and head-rice breakage while achieving uniform drying to 13% moisture.
