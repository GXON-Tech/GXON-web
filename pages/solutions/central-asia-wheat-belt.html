THE CHALLENGE

Short Window, Massive Volume

Central Asia's continental climate compresses wheat harvest into 2-3 weeks — requiring massive drying capacity that runs day and night to prevent losses.

2-3 Weeks to Process Everything

Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and southern Russia experience a short, intense wheat harvest. Continental climate means the harvest window is compressed to 14-21 days. During this window, farms and grain depots must process 2,000+ tons per day. Any drying bottleneck means grain sits in fields or trucks — at risk of rain, pests, and quality degradation.

Unlike subtropical regions with extended harvest seasons, Central Asian wheat must be moved from field to storage almost immediately. A delay of even one day can expose thousands of tons to sudden autumn storms or early frost. The drying system must operate at full capacity around the clock for the entire harvest window.

Energy Infrastructure Drives Fuel Choice

Central Asia's energy infrastructure varies by location. Kazakhstan has abundant natural gas. Uzbekistan has both gas and coal. Remote areas may only have coal access. The dryer must adapt to the local energy mix — switching between natural gas (cleaner, cheaper where available) and coal (universally available) without major reconfiguration.

Single-fuel dryers force operators into expensive fuel transport or impossible infrastructure projects. Dual-fuel capability lets each facility use whatever is locally abundant — minimizing operating cost and ensuring uninterrupted operation even when one fuel supply is disrupted.

HOW IT WORKS

The Drying Process

From combine harvester to storage-ready wheat in five continuous automated steps.

1
Bulk Intake
Wheat delivered from combine harvesters via truck. Intake pit handles 200+ TPD without bottlenecks.
2
Continuous Drying
Grain descends through 3 temperature zones (70-100°C). Each zone optimized for wheat drying curve.
3
Multi-Zone Control
PLC adjusts zone temperatures based on incoming moisture and discharge moisture readings. Ensures uniform final moisture.
4
Quality Check
Discharge moisture sensor verifies wheat at 13-14% storage moisture. Auto-adjusts residence time if needed.
5
Storage/Reserve
Dried wheat conveyed to storage silos or government reserve facilities. Meets grain reserve quality standards.

FAQ

Common Questions

Can the dryer switch between natural gas and coal?
Yes. The dual-fuel furnace supports both natural gas and coal. Switching takes approximately 2 hours and requires changing the burner module. Gas is recommended where pipeline infrastructure exists; coal for remote locations.
How does the system handle winter harvest conditions?
The G-CT-200 is designed for Central Asian winters. The tower is fully enclosed with insulated walls. Pre-heating system warms incoming air before it enters the heat exchanger. Operational down to -20°C ambient temperature.
Does it meet government grain reserve standards?
Yes. The system produces grain with ±1% moisture uniformity and indirect heat drying — meeting government grain reserve quality requirements. Automatic documentation includes moisture logs, temperature records, and throughput data for compliance.
How long does installation take?
25-35 days for full installation including foundation, tower erection, electrical, and commissioning. Civil works (foundation) typically take 2 weeks. GXON provides on-site installation supervision and operator training.
What maintenance is required during the harvest season?
Daily: inspect fuel lines and clean dust filters. Every 3 days: clean heat exchanger surfaces. Weekly: lubricate elevator chains and check belt tension. Post-season: full system inspection and winterization.
Can it dry grains other than wheat?
Yes. While optimized for wheat, the PLC supports profiles for barley, rapeseed, and sunflower. The multi-zone system adapts drying parameters for each grain type.
