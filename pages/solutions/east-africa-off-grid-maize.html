THE CHALLENGE

When the Grid Doesn't Reach

Rural East African farming regions lack reliable grid power — leaving maize to sun-dry with devastating losses during seasonal rains.

No Power, No Drying

In rural Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, and Uganda, less than 30% of farming communities have reliable grid electricity. Without power, mechanical drying is impossible. Maize harvested at 20–25% moisture must be sun-dried on tarpaulins — a process that takes 5–7 days and fails completely during seasonal rains.

Cooperative Scale, Individual Need

Smallholder farmers typically cultivate 1–3 hectares. Individually, they cannot justify purchasing a dryer. But organized into cooperatives of 50–100 farmers, a shared mobile dryer serving multiple villages by rotation becomes economically viable. The challenge is making the dryer work off-grid and keeping fuel costs affordable.

HOW IT WORKS

The Off-Grid Drying Process

From generator start to village rotation in five steps — built for cooperative operation.

1
Generator Start
Diesel generator powers up. Auto-idle conserves fuel between batches.
2
Load Maize
Cooperative members deliver maize at 20–25% moisture. 10-ton batch loaded via elevator.
3
Biomass Drying
Biomass pellet burner heats air to 55–60°C. PLC controls cycle (4–5 hours per batch).
4
Moisture Check
Sensor verifies maize reached 13–14% safe storage moisture. Auto-stop prevents over-drying.
5
Rotate
Unload dry maize, tow dryer to next village. One dryer serves 3–5 villages per season.

FAQ

Common Questions

What size generator do I need?
A 15 kVA diesel generator is included with the package. It is sized to run the dryer, elevator, and control system with margin for startup surges.
Where do I source biomass pellets?
Pellets can be made locally from maize cobs, stalks, and husks using a small pellet mill (available separately). Alternatively, commercial pellets are available in most East African countries.
How does the cooperative rotation model work?
The dryer is towed between 3–5 villages on a rotation schedule. Each village hosts the dryer for 3–5 days during peak harvest. GXON provides a scheduling template and training for cooperative managers.
What maintenance is required?
Daily: clean dust filters, check generator oil. Weekly: lubricate elevator chain, inspect belts. Annually: full burner service, generator service, heat exchanger inspection.
Can the dryer handle other grains?
Yes. The PLC supports drying profiles for maize, sorghum, beans, and wheat. Switch grain type via the control panel.
Is operator training included?
Yes. GXON provides 3-day on-site training covering operation, maintenance, and basic troubleshooting. Training materials in English and Swahili.
